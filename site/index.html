Find the jobs that fit your skills, without handing your data to anyone.

myJobs reads every posting for the skills it asks for and scores it against the skills you have and the ones you want to use. The list, the scores, your CV and your applications all stay on your computer. There is no account and nothing to sign up for.

A fit score you can check by hand

No model picks your jobs. The score is plain arithmetic over skills, so the same postings and the same profile always give the same list, and every number on a card can be traced back.

  1. You rate your skills

    Every skill gets two numbers from 0 to 10: My expertise, how much of it you can show, and Goal importance, how much the job you want needs it. Import them from your CV, LinkedIn or JSON Resume, then adjust.

  2. Each posting is read for skills

    myJobs finds the skills a posting asks for, from a shared dictionary of technical skills, tools, industry knowledge, leadership and personal traits. Each is weighted by how critical that kind of skill is: a core technical skill counts more than a soft trait.

  3. Two scores, out of 100

    The posting's 100 points are split over the skills it asks for. Paid with your expertise, that is the fit; paid with your goal importance, the goal. The card shows their average. A posting that asks for three things you rate 9 beats one that asks for twenty you don't.

Location, title and your rules (words you never want in a title or description) only filter. They never add or subtract points. A posting with no description gets no fit score rather than a made-up one.

The tools

Every screenshot here comes from a made-up demo profile.

My Career: your skills

One table per kind of skill, each with your two numbers. Change one and the whole list re-scores at once.

Fill it from a LinkedIn profile PDF, a JSON Resume, or your own CV. To read a CV in depth, myJobs writes a prompt: you paste it and your CV into any chat assistant you like, then paste the answer back. myJobs itself sends nothing.

Your positions, education and certifications live here too, and feed the CV builder.

CV & ATS check

Open a posting and check your CV against it. myJobs reads your CV file (Word, PDF or Markdown) and shows:

  • which of the posting's skills your CV mentions, weighted by how much the posting needs each one, with a grade from A to D
  • a step-by-step plan to raise the match, biggest gain first, with a reminder to add only what is true
  • skills that appear only in a skills list and never in your work history
  • whether the job title appears in your CV, because recruiters search by title
  • file checks that resume parsers document: contact details, section headings, date ranges

This shows what keyword matching sees. It does not predict an outcome.

My Skills

Your skills table, each with two numbers: your expertise and how much you want it. Beside it, what the postings for your professions and countries ask for most, next to what you have: the top technical skills, industry knowledge, spoken languages and work modes.

A gap here is a concrete next thing to learn. One button copies a prompt with your skills and these gaps for any chat assistant, asking for learning steps.

CV builder

Turn My Career into a CV. Pick a template, choose which positions and sections go in, and save a PDF. Each template says whether applicant tracking systems read it safely. Copy a template and edit its HTML and CSS if you want your own.

Market

Every posting myJobs has pulled is kept, with the day it appeared and the day it closed. The Market screen shows which skills employers ask for and how that share is trending, next to your own two numbers, plus how many jobs are posted and how long they stay open.

My Applications

Mark a job as applied and it moves here. The same vacancy posted in several places counts once. Attach the CV and cover letter you sent; myJobs keeps a copy as it was that day.

If you connect your mailbox, myJobs reads it over IMAP with an app password you create and matches replies to the applications they answer. It is off until you set it up.

Your CVs, cover letters and certificates sit in My Documents, in plain folders next to the app.

Your data stays on your computer

myJobs is one file. Put it in a folder, run it, and everything it creates goes in that folder: your skills, your CVs, your applications, the postings it pulled. Back it up, move it to another computer or delete it. There is no myJobs server and no account. Nothing about you is sent to us.

No telemetry

No analytics, no crash reports, no usage counts. This website has no tracking either.

No AI in the scoring

Fetching and scoring use no model. The optional prompts (reading a CV, learning steps) are text you copy into a chat assistant of your choice, when you choose.

Your profile never leaves

Postings are downloaded first and scored afterwards, on your machine. Career sites and job boards never see your skills, your CV or what you are looking for.

What it connects to

  • Career sites and job boards, to fetch postings when you press Sync.
  • GitHub, for updates: once a day it reads a small signed version file. A new version of the app is downloaded only when you click Install.
  • Your own mailbox, only if you set it up.
  • Claude Code, only if you turn it on: an optional check after Sync for sources that broke, through your own install and account. Off by default.

First launch

Put the file in an empty folder of its own, such as myJobs. When it starts, it creates its data next to itself and opens the dashboard. The files aren't code-signed yet, so your system will warn you the first time:

Windows

SmartScreen says "Windows protected your PC". Click More info, then Run anyway.

macOS

In Terminal, from the folder:

chmod +x myjobs-darwin-arm64
xattr -d com.apple.quarantine myjobs-darwin-arm64
./myjobs-darwin-arm64

Or, in Finder, right-click the file and choose Open. On macOS 15 and later, try opening it once, then go to System Settings, Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. On an Intel Mac, use myjobs-darwin-x64.

Linux

chmod +x myjobs-linux-x64
./myjobs-linux-x64

After that, myJobs checks for updates itself and shows new versions in its notification bell. It never installs one without asking.
